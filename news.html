Convention message · 2025 archive

The supplied article is dated 16 September 2025. The convention is presented here as a historical post, not as an upcoming event.

Convention welcome message

Christ’s Salvation Victory Mission International 41st Annual Convention

We give all glory to God as we welcome every one of us very warmly to this year’s annual convention.

For forty-one years, God has been faithful. We bless His holy name over the Governing Mission Council, the pastors, ministers, workers and every member of Christ’s Salvation Victory Mission International. We thank God for every child of God present at the convention.

“The voice of him that cried in the wilderness, Prepare ye the way of the Lord, make straight in the desert a highway for our God.” Isaiah 40:3

The theme of the convention was Greater Glory, drawn from Isaiah 40:3–5. The message reflected on the promise that the glory of the Lord would be revealed.

The supplied welcome message explained that the convention was decentralised, with parishes and branches meeting locally and going out for evangelism. The stated aim was to fulfil the Great Commission by sharing the gospel in streets, neighbourhoods and places where people might not ordinarily pay attention to the things of the kingdom.

Members were encouraged to speak to someone about Jesus, share their testimonies, invite people to church programmes and help others come to the saving knowledge of Jesus Christ.

The message closed with thanksgiving and prayer for the convention, its programmes, prayer sessions, evangelistic efforts and ministers, with the hope that God’s glory would be revealed in the lives of His people.

In Jesus’ mighty name, we have prayed. Amen.
Congratulations. Happy convention.

Church history · 2025 archive

Our heritage

Brief History of the Church

Christ Salvation Victory Mission International was founded in 1984 in response to a divine mandate given to Reverend Samuel Ajeje Falodun to pioneer a soul-winning ministry grounded in personal evangelism, the Word of God and prayer.

Reverend Falodun took a dignified exit from the Redeemed Christian Church of God in 1981. He worked with His Coming Evangelical Church Inc. in Ibadan until 1984, when, according to the supplied history, the instruction to begin the assignment became clear.

He began outreach in the Orile and Sari Iganmu communities, as well as Badia, Ijora and Amukoko in Lagos, sharing messages intended to bring people to Christ.

Reverend Samuel Ajeje Falodun passed away on 12 October 1990. The young ministry was left in the hands of Elder Victor Olufemi Fasehun, who led the church for three years. On 31 October 1993, he handed over leadership to Pastor Emmanuel Olu Falodun.

Pastor Emmanuel Oluwemimo Falodun was ordained as Senior Pastor on 9 November 1998 by Pastor E. A. Adeboye, General Overseer of the Redeemed Christian Church of God.

The church states its continuing commitment to raising Christian leaders, planting vibrant churches and sharing the gospel until the coming of Jesus Christ.

This summary follows the historical account supplied by the church.

Community impact · 2025 archive

Ministry beyond the walls

Community Impact

CSVM engages in spiritual mapping and territory-taking through its Annual All Believers Fasting and Prayer, a 21-day programme that dates back to 1994.

According to the supplied account, the programme has extended internationally with the help of the internet and social media, allowing participants in different locations to join. The church says it prepares members with sustained chain prayers and fasting for 100 days before the public programme.

The annual programme takes place between mid-October and the first Sunday of November. Visitors are encouraged by the church to check official channels for updates before participating.

Beyond prayer and evangelism, the church reports providing free health checks and medical services to rural communities in Ogun State and donating food and clothing items.

The supplied account also describes the church’s involvement in Ajerogun Town’s electrification project, including assistance with an application for support to the Ogun State Government and follow-up until the supply of a 500KVA transformer to the State Government was achieved.

Victory Camp

The church describes Victory Camp as a place of prayer, power and the presence of the Almighty God. Learn more about the camp and its documented support needs on the About Us page.
